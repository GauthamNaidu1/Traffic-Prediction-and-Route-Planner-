  $$\text{Base Multiplier} = \text{clip}\left(0.85 + 1.60 \times \frac{V}{5830}, \; 0.85, \; 2.50\right)$$

## 10. Technical Interview Questions & Honest Answers

### Question 1: Why did you use Gradient Boosting if it did not statistically outperform the historical mean lookup table?
**Answer**:
> "On this single-sensor dataset with calendar-only inputs, the historical mean by (hour, day of week) is an extraordinarily strong non-parametric estimator because traffic follows regular diurnal work and weekend cycles. Our paired bootstrap hypothesis test proved that Gradient Boosting achieved a difference of only $-0.07$ veh/h with a 95% confidence interval of $[-0.23, +0.09]$ veh/h, which overlaps zero. 
> 
> However, building the pipeline with Gradient Boosting was critical for two technical reasons:
> 1. Extensibility: A static lookup table cannot naturally ingest continuous or high-cardinality exogenous signals without severe curse-of-dimensionality binning. As demonstrated in our weather nowcasting experiment, tree ensembles smoothly absorbed continuous precipitation and temperature telemetry to lower MAE to 253.46 veh/h.
> 2. Model Size: The trained tree model serialized to 0.28 MB with sub-millisecond inference, making it an efficient continuous function approximator for the deployed Streamlit application."

---

### Question 2: Why did your downstream routing simulation report 0.0% route changes across 1,200 queries?
**Answer**:
> "The 0.0% route change share is an honest, mathematically verified property of the evaluation graph's topology. Our road network is a hand-built network of 23 nodes and 26 edges forming only 4 planar cycles. On this network, alternative detour paths are approximately 50% longer in base physical distance than direct paths.
> 
> Because our volume-to-delay formula scales edge multipliers uniformly across road hierarchy classes under a city-wide congestion assumption, the congestion penalty on direct links never exceeded the 50% geometric penalty required to make a detour optimal. This finding is valuable because it illustrates that dynamic routing only changes topological paths when a network has high redundancy with parallel corridors of comparable base travel times."

---

### Question 3: Why did you separate calendar forecasting from weather nowcasting?
**Answer**:
> "We separated them to avoid operational data leakage and preserve causal integrity. When a traveler schedules a route in advance (e.g., planning a commute for next Friday at 08:00), exact contemporaneous weather observations (such as 1-hour rainfall or cloud cover) do not exist. Assuming known future weather at model inference time would introduce an unrealistic information advantage.
> 
> Therefore, we strictly limited the deployed operational model to calendar features (`hour_sin`, `hour_cos`, `day_of_week`, `is_weekend`, `is_holiday`), which are deterministic and known indefinitely in advance. We restricted weather features to an offline nowcasting benchmark, allowing us to quantify the exact marginal value of sensor telemetry (~7.28 veh/h MAE improvement) without invalidating the deployment assumptions."
